The Psychology

Why cash hits different.

Researcher Diya Samit connects overspending to the "Pain of Paying" — the discomfort people feel handing over physical money. With cash, that loss is visible. With UPI, the transaction completes in under a second. The money stops feeling real.

Dhivya, Raj Kumar, and Pratiksha found that cashback rewards further reduce spending friction — delivering instant satisfaction that makes impulsive purchases even more likely.

"Without UPI, I wouldn't have spent as much ordering food online. The money doesn't feel the same as cash."

— Interview participant · Dev, Gupta & Kumar, CHI 2024

The Rise

From 2.65M
to 108 billion.

Launched in 2016 by NPCI and RBI, UPI grew 1,100% in five years. Demonetization forced mass adoption overnight, and an open architecture let PhonePe and Google Pay build on top — with India capping any single app at 30% market share to prevent monopolization (Mathew et al.; Cornelli et al.).

2016

Launch + Demonetization

NPCI & RBI launch UPI. Cash abolished overnight.

The government's sudden removal of ₹500 and ₹1,000 notes forced hundreds of millions to find a digital alternative. UPI, having just launched, filled that vacuum — starting with just 2.65 million transactions.

2017–2020

PhonePe & GPay Enter

Open architecture. 30% market cap enforced.

Unlike China's closed platforms, India's open UPI infrastructure allowed competition. A government rule capping any single company at 30% of transactions keeps the ecosystem balanced (Cornelli et al.).

2024

12 Billion Monthly Transactions

108B projected annually. India's default payment layer.

A 1,100% jump in five years. UPI now processes more transactions than Visa and Mastercard combined in India, and is expanding internationally — accepted in Singapore, UAE, France, and more (Mathew et al.).

Financial Inclusion

Bringing millions into banking.

UPI brought rural and semi-urban Indians into formal banking for the first time — enabling savings, digital commerce, and economic participation where card machines were never affordable (Haque et al.; Garg & Agarwal).

🏦

24/7 Banking Access

Instant real-time transfers through one app — critical for rural areas without bank branches (Haque et al.).

🛡️

Strong Privacy Protection

Strict rules prevent third-party apps from collecting transaction data, protecting hundreds of millions (Cornelli et al.).

🏪

Small Business Growth

Merchants who couldn't afford card machines now accept payments via a free QR code (Cornelli et al.).

📊

Digital Financial Records

Every transaction is logged — giving low-income users a financial history to access loans and credit (Haque et al.).

The Paradox

They know. They still keep using it.

If UPI increases overspending, why do hundreds of millions continue using it daily? Kiran and Sailaja studied this through the UTAUT model — four factors that drive continued adoption even when users recognize the downsides.

Social influence is especially powerful: UPI is so dominant that for many users it is no longer a choice — it is the only accepted payment method. Cash refusal, like what I personally experienced visiting India, makes opting out impossible.

"62.3% of users adopted UPI only because people around them — family, friends, shop owners — had already adopted it."

— Samit · SSRN 2025

What You Can Do

Your data is a mirror.

UPI isn't the problem — invisibility is. Researchers recommend platforms integrate financial tools into apps, and that users treat transaction history as a decision-making tool (Dev et al.; Samit).

01

Weekly review

Spend 5 minutes every Sunday reviewing your UPI history. Dev et al. found simply seeing patterns creates behavioral change.

02

Set a monthly cap

Most UPI apps support budget limits. Dev et al. recommend this as a key feature platforms should actively promote.

03

The 10-second pause

Recreate Samit's "pain of paying" deliberately — pause before unplanned purchases. The discomfort is intentional.

04

Categorize spending

Apps like Fi or Jupiter auto-categorize UPI spends, making patterns visible — exactly what Samit recommends for awareness.
